01 / About

From analyzing data to building with it.

I like problems where the answer is hiding. In fraud and anomaly detection, the cases that matter are rare and usually look perfectly normal until you look closer. That's the kind of puzzle I keep coming back to.

My first master's was mostly about analyzing data. Working with it every day, I realized the part I enjoyed most was the building: the code, the pipelines, how things actually work underneath. So I reskilled. At the ECB I went from intern to business analyst, then into data engineering and backend development in Python, before going back to university for a second master's in Data Science and AI at TU/e. Now I'm at ING for my thesis, studying how uncertainty changes what we can actually say about fairness in AML risk models.

Because I've built the pipelines and APIs that models end up living on, I think about the whole path: messy data in, something reliable running in production out.

A multilingual NLP pipeline that reads accommodation reviews for safety concerns, combining zero-shot classification with a fine-tuned mDeBERTa model to score how serious each one is. It surfaced highly rated properties that were hiding real safety incidents.
